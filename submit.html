SUBMIT A PATCH

Show us your patch.

Maximalist is for final projects made in Max. This page covers what to send so your work looks right in the gallery and anyone can open your patch afterwards.

Skip to the form ↓

What to send

REQUIRED

The patch

Your .maxpat file. If it uses samples, videos, images or abstractions, zip the whole project folder instead. Try opening it on a different computer first so you know nothing is missing.

STRONGLY RECOMMENDED

A patching view screenshot

Unlock the patch so every object box and cord is showing, then take a screenshot. PNG, at least 1600px wide. Messy is fine. This is what shows people how it was actually built.
Optional: take a second one with Max set to a dark theme. The site shows it when someone switches to dark mode.

REQUIRED

A still of it running

What the audience sees: presentation mode, the Jitter window, or a photo of the installation. JPG or PNG, at least 1600px on the long side. Vertical and square are fine, the site handles both.

OPTIONAL

A preview

A short recording of the patch running.
Video: MP4 (H.264), 10 to 30 seconds, under 50 MB.
Audio: MP3, under 2 minutes, under 10 MB.

REQUIRED

A description and an artist statement

Two short pieces of writing in your own words. The description says what the patch does and how. The statement says what it’s about and why you made it. Examples below.

THE SMALL STUFF

Details

Your name, the title, tags (sound, visual, interactive, 3D, other), and a link to your portfolio if you have one. Name files like lastname_title.maxpat.

Writing your description and statement

Say what it does and how. Name the objects doing the heavy lifting. Skip the big words. If something didn’t work the way you planned, it’s okay to say that too, it helps whoever opens your patch next.

TOO VAGUE

“An immersive exploration of sound and light that invites viewers to reflect on their own presence in the space.”

BETTER

“A webcam feeds jit.grab into a feedback loop. When the room gets loud, peakamp~ pushes the zoom up, so the image eats itself faster. I wanted the sensitivity higher but it kept glitching out.”

Your artist statement is the other half: what the piece is about, what you were exploring, and what you want people to notice. A few sentences is plenty. It’s fine to be personal.

TOO VAGUE

“This piece explores the relationship between technology and humanity.”

BETTER

“I’m always loud without noticing. I wanted a room that shows you how much space your voice takes up, so the louder you are the more the image falls apart.”

Keep it real

This site is supposed to show what people actually made in Max. A few rules keep it that way:

  • Every image and recording has to come from your patch actually running. No generated or stock images as your cover.
  • Write the description and statement yourself. They don’t have to be fancy.
  • If you used AI anywhere, for help with code or for media inside the piece, say so on the form. Reviewers look at it case by case. We just want people to know what they’re looking at.
  • The patch file is required, and the patching screenshot is strongly recommended. People should be able to see how it was built.

What happens after you submit

  1. You send the form with links to your files.
  2. A reviewer checks it. They open the patch, make sure the files work and the credits are right.
  3. It goes up, or you get a note. Approved work shows up in the gallery. If something is missing you’ll hear what to fix.
